An Android training tracker that scores what you actually lift. Log a workout,
get a number that means something.

Most trackers add up kilograms. That rewards being heavy and rewards machines
that hide how much load you are really moving, so the number you chase drifts
away from the training that earned it.

Ironvellum scores a set by what it costs you:

- Body-scaled: a rep is worth reps × (bodyweight + added) / bodyweight^0.67,
  the standard allometric exponent, so a lighter lifter is not permanently
  outranked for being light.
- Priced per implement: a marked kilogram is not the same load on every
  machine. A 45 degree sled transmits 0.70, a smith bar 0.90, a pinned stack
  0.85, a dual pulley 0.50. The plate you see is converted to the load you
  actually carry.
- Difficulty-weighted: a handstand push-up and a push-up are not the same rep.
  Tiers come from the skill tree the app already ships.
- Per-sex standards: strength score is normalised by muscle group using
  published gaps, and every strength deed states the bar for the lifter
  reading it rather than the men's number with a footnote.

Everything works offline. An account is optional and only ever backs up
training.

Features:

- Guided first run: pick your split, weekly volume, equipment and goal, and the
  app proposes an editable routine built from the real catalogue. No routine is
  imposed on you.
- Evidence-based program builder: full body, upper/lower and push/pull/legs
  templates for strength or muscle, fitted to your weekly volume and the gear
  you own, from a full gym to a pull-up bar and one dumbbell; a generated
  routine or single workout; or an improved version of a workout you already
  have, shown before and after. Cap the exercises per workout (5 by default)
  or keep to compound and skill work only. Every exercise explains why it was
  picked, citing 2020-2026 research.
- Weekly muscle coverage: a body map of the sets each muscle gets, planned or
  from the last seven days, with a floor for the helper muscles your other
  exercises mostly train. Lever and planche holds count. Each muscle's sets
  break down by exercise, and every exercise shows the muscles it works, on
  the live workout screen too.
- Live workouts: type a set's exact load, watch a running workout timer, and
  continue a workout you left. Time estimates learn your pace from the
  workouts you log. After a workout, pick which changes go back into your
  routine.
- 241 exercises: barbell, dumbbell, cable, plate-loaded, selectorised, smith,
  assisted, bodyweight, plus cardio, sport, climbing, water and mobility.
- 106-technique skill tree: fourteen lines, each rung gated on the one before
  it and carrying a written claim standard.
- 105 deeds: level, volume, streak, strength and activity milestones, with
  progress you can watch rather than a surprise.
- Progressive overload: strength and hypertrophy schools with their own rep
  bands, per-exercise load steps and a stall rule that deloads instead of
  repeating a failed workout.
- Measurements and health: weight, body fat, BMI and FFMI over time; optional
  Health Connect read for steps, distance, energy and sleep.
- Shareable workouts: a plain text card shaped after Wordle, no link and no
  image, that states only what you did.
- Optional cloud: sign in to back up training and see a feed and leaderboard.
  Body measurements and health data never leave the device. Delete your cloud
  account from the app at any time.

Ironvellum is free software under GPL-3.0-or-later. It has no ads, no trackers
and no paid tier. Source: https://github.com/AlexMollard/Ironvellum
