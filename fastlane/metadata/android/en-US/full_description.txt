An Android training tracker that scores what you actually lift. Seal a trial,
get a number that means something.

Most trackers add up kilograms. That rewards being heavy and rewards machines
that hide how much load you are really moving, so the number you chase drifts
away from the training that earned it.

Ironvellum scores a set by what it costs you:

- Body-scaled: a rep is worth reps × (bodyweight + added) / bodyweight^0.67,
  the standard allometric exponent, so a lighter body is not permanently
  outranked for being light.
- Priced per implement: a marked kilogram is not the same load on every
  machine. A 45 degree sled transmits 0.70, a smith bar 0.90, a pinned stack
  0.85, a dual pulley 0.50. The plate you see is converted to the load you
  actually carry.
- Difficulty-weighted: a handstand push-up and a push-up are not the same rep.
  Tiers come from the Paths, the technique tree the app already ships.
- Per-sex standards: strength score is normalised by muscle group using
  published gaps, and every strength deed states the bar for the person
  reading it rather than the men's number with a footnote.

Everything works offline. An account is optional and only ever backs up
training.

The Ledger keeps its own words. A saved workout is a rite, your weekly plan
is a cycle, and a workout you perform is a trial, sealed when you finish and
written into your chronicle.

Features:

- The Binding, a guided first run: pick your split, weekly volume, armoury
  (the equipment you own) and goal, and the app proposes an editable cycle
  built from the real catalogue. No cycle is imposed on you.
- The Forge, an evidence-based builder: full body, upper/lower and
  push/pull/legs patterns for strength or muscle, fitted to your weekly volume
  and your armoury, from a full gym to a pull-up bar and one dumbbell; a
  forged cycle or single rite; or a tempered version of a rite you already
  have, shown before and after. Cap the exercises per rite (5 by default) or
  keep to compound and technique work only. Every exercise explains why it was
  picked, citing 2020-2026 research.
- Weekly muscle coverage: an anatomical body map, male or female to match
  your profile, of the sets each muscle gets, planned or from the last seven
  days. Swipe between muscle tiles, gaps first, and exercise cards, and tap a
  muscle on any figure to light it with a line of context. Helper muscles your
  other exercises mostly train get a floor instead of a range. Lever and
  planche holds count. Every exercise and technique has a swipeable info sheet
  showing the muscles it works, on the live trial screen too.
- Live trials: type a set's exact load, watch a running timer, and continue a
  trial you left. Time estimates learn your pace from the trials you seal.
  After a trial, pick which changes go back into your cycle.
- 256 exercises: barbell, dumbbell, cable, plate-loaded, selectorised, smith,
  assisted, bodyweight, plus cardio, sport, climbing, water and mobility.
- 119 techniques on fourteen paths, shown as game-style nodes, each gated on
  the one before it and carrying a written claim standard.
- 105 deeds: level, volume, oath (days kept in a row), strength and activity
  milestones, with progress you can watch rather than a surprise.
- Progressive overload: strength and hypertrophy schools with their own rep
  bands, per-exercise load steps and a stall rule that deloads instead of
  repeating a failed trial.
- The Ledger: scrubbable charts for weight, body fat, BMI and FFMI, a training
  calendar and lift records; optional Health Connect read (weight, body fat,
  resting heart rate, steps, distance, energy and sleep), including past
  history and a background refresh while the app is closed.
- Shareable trials: a plain text card shaped after Wordle, no link and no
  image, that states only what you did.
- Optional cloud: sign in to back up training, follow your allies' tidings,
  form a circle with a weekly goal and stand in the Reckoning, a leaderboard for each lift. Body measurements and
  health data never leave the device. Delete your cloud account from the app
  at any time.

Ironvellum is free software under GPL-3.0-or-later. It has no ads, no trackers
and no paid tier. Source: https://github.com/AlexMollard/Ironvellum
